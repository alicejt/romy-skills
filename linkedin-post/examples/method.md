# Example: The Method

## Input

```
/linkedin-post How I plan a week of teaching. Sunday 40 mins, three passes. Stops me over preparing lessons that get cut anyway.
format: method
```

## Output

Three passes, forty minutes, every Sunday. This is how I plan a teaching week without over-preparing lessons that get cut on Wednesday.

Pass one, ten minutes. List every lesson the week actually contains, including the ones that will be interrupted by assemblies, trips and cover. Write the interruptions in first. Most over-planning happens because people plan the timetable rather than the week that will really occur.

Pass two, twenty minutes. For each lesson write one sentence: what a student should be able to do at the end that they could not do at the start. One sentence, no resources, no activities. If you cannot write the sentence, the lesson does not have a point yet and no amount of slide-making will give it one.

Pass three, ten minutes. Only now choose materials, and only for the next two days. Anything further out gets a placeholder. Thursday will not look like you imagined it on Sunday, and every resource you build for it before Tuesday is a coin flip.

The result is roughly a third of the Sunday I used to lose, and fewer beautifully prepared lessons abandoned halfway because the objective was never clear.

The mistake this avoids is treating preparation as proof of care. The sentence in pass two is the care. The slides are logistics.

---

**Format used:** The Method. A repeatable process specific enough to apply on Sunday, which is what makes it worth saving.
