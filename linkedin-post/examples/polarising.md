# Example: The Polarising Statement

## Input

```
/linkedin-post Take home coding tests are worse than pair programming interviews and most teams keep them for the wrong reasons.
format: polarising
```

## Output

Take-home coding tests select for people with free evenings, not people who can do the job.

The stated reason for using them is fairness. Everyone gets the same brief, nobody is judged on how they perform under pressure, and the work can be assessed without the interviewer's mood affecting the result. All of that is genuinely true.

The unstated reason is cheaper for us. A take-home costs the candidate four to eight hours and costs the hiring team twenty minutes of review. We have designed a process that moves the expense onto the person with the least power in the exchange, and then described it as the considerate option.

Look at who it filters out. Anyone with caring responsibilities. Anyone working notice at a demanding job. Anyone interviewing at five places, because five take-homes is a working week they are not being paid for. None of those correlate with engineering ability, and all of them correlate with the diversity numbers most teams say they care about.

Pair programming interviews cost the team more and the candidate less. That is the actual trade, and it is the reason the switch is rare.

The strongest counterargument is that pairing disadvantages people who freeze when observed, and it does. The fix for that is telling candidates the exact shape of the session in advance, not shifting eight unpaid hours onto them.

---

**Format used:** The Polarising Statement. A claim half the room would argue with, evidence, and the strongest objection answered.
