# Example: The Before/After

## Input

```
/linkedin-post Used to run every retro myself as engineering lead. Handed it to a rotating facilitator. Team started raising real problems.
format: before-after
```

## Output

For two years I ran every retrospective myself. The team was polite, the actions were sensible, and almost nothing structural ever came up.

I read that as a good sign. Calm retros, functional team. I told myself we had built enough trust that problems surfaced early elsewhere, so the meeting had nothing dramatic to do.

Then I went on leave and someone else facilitated. In one session the team raised three things I had never heard: that the on-call rota was quietly wrecking two people's weeks, that nobody understood how work got prioritised, and that my habit of picking up urgent tickets myself was reading as a lack of confidence in them.

None of that was new information to the team. It was new to me. I had been the reason it stayed unsaid, because the person who owns the process is the person you do not criticise in front of.

Now facilitation rotates. I attend, I answer questions, and I do not steer. The retros are less comfortable and considerably more useful.

The thing I would tell myself two years ago is that a quiet retrospective is not evidence of a healthy team. It is evidence that the meeting is not safe enough to be loud, and if you are running it, you are the most likely reason.

---

**Format used:** The Before/After. One specific change, an honest before, and a measurable difference.
