# Email

Identify the relationship and job before drafting: reply, introduction, request,
follow-up, update, boundary, apology, or decision. Be direct about the ask,
answer, or next step without explaining the recipient's own role back to them.

Use personal warmth only when it is true and specific. Do not flatter someone by
restating their biography or manufacture familiarity. Keep the email as short as
the job permits, but retain the reasoning or evidence needed to make a decision.

For a boundary or decline, do not undermine it with unnecessary self-deprecation
or a vague "if helpful" escape hatch. State the decision plainly and offer an
alternative only when one is real.
