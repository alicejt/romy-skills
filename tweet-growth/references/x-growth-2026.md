# X growth: operating reference

Use this reference to plan and review posts for the user and their work. It is a
diagnostic, not a promise about how X will rank an individual post.

## What not to claim

Recommendation systems predict a range of positive and negative actions. Do not
claim that a reply, bookmark, link, paid subscription, or posting time has a
fixed multiplier or guarantees reach.

Do not treat an external link as categorically forbidden. Compare direct-link
and first-reply placement against this account's own results when it can be
measured. Topic, creative, audience, and commercial objective all matter.

## Practical growth review

For a commercial post, trace the most relevant path:

`stop -> consume -> redistribute -> investigate profile -> follow, visit, or convert`

No post has to complete the whole path. A joke, reply, or personal post may do
its job at *consume*. Do not reverse-engineer every draft around a reusable
phrase or profile action; an account needs range and a human reason to follow it.

Useful inputs include:

- A recognisable audience and topical territory when the post needs one.
- An immediate, concrete payoff in the first visible line or frame.
- Original proof, a specific observation, a named concept, or a story that can
  travel beyond the original post.
- A reason to repost or quote, not merely like.
- A natural next action: conversation, profile visit, follow, deeper reading,
  or a relevant destination.
- A claim robust enough to survive scrutiny at much greater reach.

Originality with derivative potential is stronger than mechanical tricks. Name a
phenomenon, show an artefact, offer a reusable phrase, or make a story that
others can participate in. Treat these as options rather than a checklist.

## Formats

Choose based on the idea, then compare results on this account.

| Format | Best use | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short text | A strong view, joke, prediction, reaction, or observation | Generic insight, over-compressed aphorism, or a fabricated hook |
| Long text | A story, nuanced argument, or mini-case study | Length without new information |
| Thread | Modular claims/examples where each post can stand alone | Many posts containing little distinct value |
| Image/video | Product proof, demonstration, visual data, transformation | Delaying value with branding or setup |
| Reply/quote | Evidence, counterexample, implementation detail, or a funny extension | Generic praise, self-promotion, or outrage farming |
| Poll/Space/Community | Research, concentrated topical discussion, or relationship depth | Treating the format as a ranking loophole |

## Network and timing

Use Lists to find relevant practitioners, analysts, journalists, competitors,
and emerging voices before a conversation becomes saturated. Add unusually useful
replies to a small number of relevant discussions. Human, contextual replies
are durable; repetitive or automated reply tactics are policy and distribution
risks.

Start timing experiments with the audience's likely active windows, then replace
generic advice with account results. Compare posts by topic, format, audience
conditions, and time window. Post frequently enough to learn, never so often
that quality falls or the feed becomes repetitive.

## Measure a funnel

Use matched historical posts, not internet-average engagement benchmarks.

| Objective | Primary measure | Supporting signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distribution | Impressions relative to comparable recent posts | First-hour distribution |
| Virality | Reposts plus quotes divided by impressions | Shares and downstream quoting |
| Conversation | Replies divided by impressions | Reply quality and unique participants |
| Authority | Profile-to-follow conversion | Repeat engagers and qualified followers |
| Video | Average watch time relative to duration | Completion and qualified views |
| Visits | Click-through rate and conversion per 1,000 impressions | Landing-page conversion and lead quality |
| Leads or sales | Qualified leads or revenue attributed to the post | Conversion quality and sales-cycle evidence |

Treat any outlier threshold as the user's internal convention, not an X
definition. Compare medians across similar posts before drawing a conclusion.

## Experiment safely

Choose one primary metric and one variable per test: hook, format, proof,
placement, timing, or distribution route. Compare matched posts over a series.
Do not A/B test organic X by reposting near-identical copy.

Separate reach, virality, and business value. A contrarian post may earn reach
while attracting low-quality followers or weak commercial outcomes.

## Integrity

Negative feedback and recommendation eligibility matter. Avoid misinformation,
rage bait, personal pile-ons, recycled work, engagement bait, bulk duplication,
irrelevant replies, and fake or coordinated engagement.

For a disputed factual claim, make it specific enough to test, show relevant
evidence, and include the strongest counterargument when it helps. Correct a
wrong claim. If the post attracts harassment, protect people rather than
extending the spectacle.
